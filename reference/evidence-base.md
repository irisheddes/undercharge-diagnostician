# Evidence base — what informs a diagnosis, and where it came from

A diagnosis is only as good as the knowledge it diagnoses against. This file is the
literature layer, and it is also the taxonomy's provenance audit: where each failure mode
originated, and what external grounding it has that stands independent of the builder's
own cases. The citation rule is absolute: **a diagnosis may only cite what this file holds
or what the case artifacts contain. If a claim's source cannot be verified, the claim is
not made.**

Tiers: **[A]** peer-reviewed or canonical research · **[B]** published industry data or a
professional body's formal position · **[C]** practitioner-reported, plausible but
unverified — usable as a hypothesis prompt, never as evidence in a diagnosis.

## Cognitive science

**[A] The planning fallacy** — Kahneman & Tversky (1979, "Intuitive prediction: Biases and
corrective procedures"): people underestimate their own task durations even knowing their
history, because they estimate from the inside view instead of their base rates. *F10's
engine* — and it cuts deeper than delegation: even the builder's own gut estimate runs
low; only past actuals correct it.

**[A] Anchoring** — Tversky & Kahneman (1974, "Judgment under Uncertainty," *Science*):
the first number in a negotiation pulls the final number, even when arbitrary. *F4 and
F9:* an engagement with no prepared anchor gets anchored by whoever speaks first — the
client, or an AI-drafted figure nobody grounded.

**[A] BATNA** — Fisher & Ury (*Getting to Yes*, 1981): negotiating power comes from the
best alternative to agreement. *F1:* necessity acceptance is the BATNA-less negotiation —
no walk-away price exists, and the counterparty can feel it.

**[A] The impostor phenomenon** — Clance & Imes (1978), first documented in high-achieving
women: persistent discounting of one's own demonstrated competence. *F7:* the mechanism by
which the number measures confidence instead of value.

## Industry data and professional positions

**[B] PMI, Pulse of the Profession 2018** — 52% of projects experienced scope creep, up
from 43% five years prior. *F8 is the norm, not an anomaly.* Project management further
recognizes **gold plating** — the *team* adding unrequested extras — as a distinct concept
from scope creep (client-driven growth). *Independent validation of this taxonomy's
F8/F11 split: the field found the same two modes.*

**[B] AIGA position on spec work** — the design profession's body formally holds that
speculative free work compromises both designer and client, and reflects a failure to
value professional time. *F3, codified by a professional association.*

**[B] HoneyBook gender pricing study** — female freelancers earn 26% less per project than
males despite higher average education; 82% of women would reprice given rate
transparency. *F7 and F4: self-worth pricing is measurable at population scale, and
opacity of market data sustains it.*

**[B] CB Insights startup post-mortems** (483-report corpus) — pricing and cost issues
recur as named failure causes; the root-cause reading transfers: *you price wrong because
you don't know what the customer values* (F4/F5 at startup scale). Also methodological
precedent: diagnosis of closed cases as an industry's learning instrument.

**[B] Value-pricing canon** — Ronald J. Baker (*Implementing Value Pricing*, Wiley 2010)
and Alan Weiss (*Value-Based Fees*, Wiley): the professional-services argument that
pricing effort instead of client value structurally caps the seller's share. *F5's
literature.*

## Practitioner patterns — hypothesis prompts, not evidence

**[C] The billable-hours ratio** (~60–70% of a freelancer's time is billable) — any rate
reasoned from employed-salary arithmetic underprices structurally. Check the case for the
salary-conversion error; do not quote the percentage as fact.

**[C] The effort model of pricing** — underpricing as a model failure (price as
description of effort, not prediction of value) rather than a confidence failure.
Usefully separates F5 from F7; the artifacts can distinguish them.

**[C] Salary benchmarking** — new freelancers import their employed income or other
underpricers' visible rates as reference points. F4's special case: new field, no rate
history, no market data.

## Recorded gaps

- **F2 and F6 lack verified independent grounding** — currently they stand on the
  builder's cases and distilled consulting method. Diagnoses naming them as primary say so.
- The billable-hours ratio remains practitioner-reported; no primary study verified.
- Market-rate benchmarks by field and region are deliberately absent: they belong to the
  forward-looking pre-quote check. Inside a diagnosis, "what you should have charged" is a
  prescription this tool refuses; only whether *any* anchor existed is establishable.

An archive that hides its gaps reads as complete when it isn't — the gaps stay listed
until filled.
