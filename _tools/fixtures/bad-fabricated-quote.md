DIAGNOSIS
Primary cause: You fixed the price before you had seen the work — €750 was quoted from a 20-minute look at the public site and accepted before you ever opened the backend, and the one safeguard you wrote into your own quote, "if the existing setup is in reasonable shape," was never checked and never enforced, so the price came loose from the work on day one (price before scope, F2 in the reference).

HOW I KNOW
Walking the chain backward from the invoice:

The invoice is the symptom, not the cause. €750 against roughly 55 to 56 reconstructed hours is about €13.50 an hour. Your own day rate at the time was €480 — those hours are about seven working days, €3,360 in your own money. But the same engagement drowns at any rate; the rate is not the broken link.

The execution link held. The job was "largely functional and visually complete by 27 April," live on 29 April — a two-day tail and one 30-minute fix on 4 May. No polishing nobody asked for. Gold-plating is not in this case.

The scope shifts are real, but they are not the earliest break. Twelve times the work grew — two extra projects, nine old portfolio pages rebuilt, a Press page, an Approach page, a cookie banner, dead analytics, Nina's technical list, Marc's revision call — and twelve times the record closes with the same sentence: "No revised price was discussed." That looks like the cause until you price the job with every client addition removed. That is about €36 an hour, well under the €60 an hour your own day rate implies, before Clara's first "Tiny question" ever arrived on 8 April. The engagement was underpriced before it grew. The growth compounded the loss; it did not create it.

The break is at the pricing moment, and your own emails show it happening. You priced a five-item list; she bought an outcome. Every later request — "Is it annoying to add the other 2 as well while you're in there?", "One more boring thing", "Could you do that then?" — sat comfortably inside "looking more current and functioning properly." You did not need this job, so necessity is not the cause. And while no prices were published anywhere, you held a rate and you spoke first — so this is not the unpriced offer either.
