```text
DIAGNOSIS
Primary cause: Maya began this engagement with no price of her own anywhere — no rate
card, no published prices, no idea what studio rebrands cost — so the only number that
could exist was the client's, and €900 became the price the moment it was said (F9, the
unpriced offer).

HOW I KNOW
All four artifacts are present. The hours are reconstructed, not logged — file dates
show 31 working days across 9 weeks and Maya's own estimate is "honestly? 120 hours,
maybe more" — so the depth of the loss is approximate, but at €900 flat its direction
is not in doubt. The intake answers and the artifacts do not conflict anywhere; the
testimony matches the record.

Walking the chain backward from the invoice:

- Scope shifts (F8): four additions in weeks 2–6 ("could the schedule page link to our
  booking thing?", "could you make a little welcome PDF?", "could we get some too?",
  "can we warm it up?") were all absorbed — "sure, I'll have a look!", "since I'm
  already in the brand files" — and "no message about price exists anywhere in weeks
  2–9." Broken, but downstream: with no rate and no priced scope boundary, there was
  nothing to measure any addition against.
- The agreed price: €900 flat for what became a durable, daily-used brand system, plus
  a relationship discount — warm referral, and Maya's own answer: "I'd have said more
  to a stranger. And pushed back on the extras." Broken, but the discount could only
  pass unnoticed because there was no reference price to see it against.
- The anchor: the client spoke first — "We were thinking around €900 if that works
  budget wise" — and Maya accepted in the same message: "That works!" Her intake answer
  states it plainly: "No outside number was in the room; Maya has no rate card and has
  never checked what studio rebrands cost." This is F9's evidence signature verbatim:
  the pricing moment opens with the client's number, not the freelancer's.
- Scope at pricing time: the scope was composed after the number, inside the acceptance
  itself — "For €900 I'll do the logo refresh, a small brand guide and update your site
  pages so it all matches" — two lines, never written down again. The scope was fitted
  to the client's budget, not the price to the work. That inversion is only possible
  when the freelancer arrives with no number of her own.
- The decision to take the job: finances were "tight. Two invoices unpaid... quiet
  month ahead" (F1). Real, and it explains the speed of the yes — but even a
  comfortable Maya, holding no rate and no market knowledge, had nothing to counter
  with. Necessity amplified the acceptance; it did not create the vacuum the client's
  number fell into.

The earliest broken point sits before any of these links — before this client appeared.
Cause-versus-symptom test: the tight month, the €900 figure, even the vague scope could
each have been different and the engagement still prices from whatever the client first
suggests, because no price of Maya's existed anywhere to collide with it. That standing
condition is the first fact from which everything downstream follows.

This was not a chosen strategic discount (the F6 distinction): nothing in the kit shows
a knowing decision to go in cheap, and "I stopped wanting to know" is the voice of an
unexamined discount, not a strategy.

CONTRIBUTING (not primary)
- F1 necessity acceptance — the tight month explains accepting in the same message, but
  it only amplified a negotiation Maya entered with no number of her own to defend.
- F2 price before scope — the price did precede any real scope, but the inversion
  (scope fitted to €900) was produced by the client's number arriving into a pricing
  vacuum; the vacuum came first.
- F8 silent scope growth — four unpriced additions, but there was no priced scope or
  rate in existence for a change-order to invoke.
- F6 relationship discount — "I'd have said more to a stranger," but a discount is only
  invisible when no reference price exists to discount from. (Noted per the evidence
  base: F6 currently lacks verified independent grounding.)
- F11 gold-plating — the work was "essentially done at week 6"; weeks 7–9 were spacing
  rework, "alternate logo lockups nobody asked for," and rewriting the guide intro. This
  deepened the loss on a price already broken; it did not cause it.

WHAT THIS DIAGNOSIS CANNOT TELL YOU
Whether the client had budget beyond €900 — "if that works budget wise" is the thread's
only budget signal, and whether a counter or a scoped quote would have been accepted is
not in these artifacts. The true hours — no log was kept; 120 is Maya's own reconstruction,
so the effective rate (at most €7.50/hour) is a ceiling, not a measurement. And what the
rebrand is worth to the studio — it is still in daily use a year later and the owner says
it "changed how people see the studio," but "nobody, on either side, ever named a number,"
so the size of the value left unpriced cannot be established from this kit.
```
