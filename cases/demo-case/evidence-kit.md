# Demo case — evidence kit

**This case is fictional.** It exists to demonstrate the method with a fully public
transcript, since real cases stay private. The engagement, people, and messages below
are invented; the run on them is real and unedited.

Maya, a freelance brand designer, took on a rebrand for a small yoga studio. Flat price,
agreed up front. The job ended paid in full and deeply underwater on hours.

## 1 — The pricing moment

WhatsApp, 3 March, after one 30-minute call:

> Studio owner: "We loved talking to you!! What would something like this cost? We were
> thinking around €900 if that works budget wise 🙏"
>
> Maya: "That works! For €900 I'll do the logo refresh, a small brand guide and update
> your site pages so it all matches. Can't wait to start 😊"
>
> Studio owner: "Amazing!! When can you start?"

No other pricing discussion appears anywhere in the thread. No written scope existed
before or after this exchange beyond the two lines above.

## 2 — The scope as delivered

1. Logo refresh (three concept rounds instead of the planned one)
2. Brand guide, 14 pages
3. Full site redesign, 6 pages (was "update your site pages")
4. Class schedule page with booking links to their booking system (added week 2)
5. New-student welcome PDF (added week 3, "since you're already in the brand files")
6. Instagram template set, 9 templates (added week 4)
7. Second brand guide version after the co-owner returned from a retreat and wanted
   warmer colors

## 3 — The time spent

No hour log was kept. Reconstructed from file dates and message timestamps: files
created or modified on 31 distinct days across 9 weeks. Maya's own estimate when asked
afterwards: "honestly? 120 hours, maybe more. I stopped wanting to know." At €900 flat
that is at most €7.50 per hour.

## 4 — The scope-shift record

- Week 2, owner: "could the schedule page link to our booking thing? should be easy
  right?" — Maya: "sure, I'll have a look!" No mention of price.
- Week 3, owner: "one tiny thing, new students keep asking the same questions, could you
  make a little welcome PDF?" — Maya: "haha okay, since I'm already in the brand files."
- Week 4, owner: "the templates you posted for Luna Pilates looked so nice, could we get
  some too?" — Maya: "I'll add a small set 💛"
- Week 6, co-owner returns: "I know this is late but the palette feels cold to me, can
  we warm it up?" — Maya redid the guide. No message about price exists anywhere in
  weeks 2–9.

## Answers to the standard intake questions

1. Financial situation at yes: tight. Two invoices unpaid from other clients, quiet
   month ahead.
2. Scope both sides believed at pricing time: "logo refresh, small brand guide, update
   the site pages so it matches." Nothing written beyond that.
3. Pre-agreement work: one free 30-minute call, one free "quick moodboard" sent the day
   after the call to "show the direction."
4. Who said the first number: the client (€900). Maya accepted it in the same message.
   No outside number was in the room; Maya has no rate card and has never checked what
   studio rebrands cost.
5. When work grew: see the scope-shift record. Everything was absorbed; nothing was
   re-priced.
6. Functionally done vs delivered: the site and guide were essentially done at week 6;
   weeks 7–9 were Maya reworking spacing, exporting alternate logo lockups nobody asked
   for, and rewriting the guide intro "so it reads less stiff."
7. Rate card or published prices at the start: none.
8. The client relationship: warm referral from Maya's own yoga teacher. Would a
   stranger have been quoted the same? Maya: "no, honestly. I'd have said more to a
   stranger. And pushed back on the extras."
9. Outcome value: the studio still uses everything daily, a year later. The owner told
   the referrer the rebrand "changed how people see the studio." Nobody, on either
   side, ever named a number for what it is worth.
