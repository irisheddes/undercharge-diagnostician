DIAGNOSIS

HOW I KNOW

Walking the chain backward from the invoice.

**The invoice against the hours.** The invoice was €750. The reconstructed time — "Low estimate: about 55 hours" — sits against your own standing benchmark: "His usual day rate at that time was €480". The fee is about a day and a half at that rate; fifty-five hours went in. The engagement was underpriced. The question is where it broke.

**The growth link looks broken — but the ranking test comes first.** The work grew at least ten times, each in the same shape: "Is it annoying to add the other 2 as well while you're in there?", "Would it make sense to make the old ones match too?" — and each entry in the record closes with the same line: "No revised price was discussed." That is the signature of silent scope growth (F8). But an underpriced job that also grew shows that signature regardless, so the ranking rule here is arithmetic, not judgment: deliver only the priced items, on the site as it actually was — does the price still hold?

**The test, in hours.** The priced list is in the record: "The original written scope was a homepage cleanup, mobile spacing fixes, team-page update, 3 recent projects and repair of the contact form." Your quote also covered "implementation, testing, and one round of small corrections after you review it". Delivering exactly that, on the real site, cost:

- "Estimated setup total: 4 hr 40 min" — including "Plugin conflict troubleshooting: about 1 hr 20 min", repair work the site itself demanded;
- "Original page-work total: about 11 hr 30 min" — the homepage, team page, first three projects, contact form and first mobile pass;
- "Further mobile/tablet corrections: about 3 hr 20 min" — mobile fixes were on the priced list;
- "Browser/device testing: about 1 hr 40 min" — testing was in the quote.

That is a little over 21 hours before counting a single call or message, and before the rest of the repair work nobody asked for and nobody could see from outside: "Replaced one abandoned image-slider plugin." (about 1 hr 30 min), "Fixed mixed-content warnings left from an old site migration." (about 1 hr 15 min). Call the floor 21 hours and the fair count closer to 24. Against a fee worth about a day and a half at your own rate, the priced items alone overran the price. The price was never sound, so the break sits at the pricing moment, and the growth deepened the hole rather than dug it.

**Why the pricing moment broke: the number predates the knowledge.** You quoted within hours of the first email: "If the existing setup is in reasonable shape and we're working within the current theme, I'd estimate €750 fixed for the items you listed." That sentence carries its own diagnosis — the price rested on a condition you had not yet checked. You even reserved the check: "If you send me the login and the project material, I can take a look before I start." But acceptance arrived first — "€750 is fine." — and the background record is explicit: "Before quoting €750, Elias spent about 20 minutes looking through Harbor & Field's public website." and "He did not log into the WordPress backend until after the price had already been accepted." The next morning the condition failed in plain sight — "Sending logins now. Pls ignore the mess in the backend" — and the reply was "haha noted". The price was not re-opened then, or at any of the ten openings that followed. From that moment, whatever the site actually contained — the plugin conflict, the abandoned slider, the migration debris, the dead analytics — was, by construction, free.

**The earlier link holds.** This was not a job taken out of need: "Harbor & Field was not needed to cover an immediate financial shortfall." So the decision to take the job was sound, and the earliest broken link on the chain is the scope known at pricing time.

WHAT THIS DIAGNOSIS CANNOT TELL YOU

The hours are a reconstruction, not a log — "He reconstructed the total later from Toggl, browser history, calendar entries, file timestamps and WhatsApp" — and part of the time is unrecoverable because "some of them were later deleted from the chat". The totals are floors with error in both directions. The diagnosis survives this: it rests on a 21-hour minimum for the priced items against a fee worth about a day and a half at your own rate, a gap reconstruction error of this size cannot close.

How the €750 was actually derived is not in the record — whether your day rate, a past project, or a plain guess produced the number. These artifacts show no external number was present; they cannot show the internal arithmetic.

Whether an inspection before quoting would have moved the number is unknowable from here. The record shows the inspection did not happen before the price was fixed, not what it would have concluded.

Whether the work was also underpriced against its outcome: "More than two years later, the core homepage structure, portfolio template, Press page and Approach page created during the project were still live." But nobody on either side ever named what the site earns or saves the client, so effort-priced outcome work (F5) can be neither confirmed nor ranked from these artifacts.

And one limit of the method itself: price before scope (F2) is one of the two modes in this folder's reference base that lacks verified independent grounding — it stands on the builder's own cases and distilled consulting method, and a diagnosis naming it as primary is required to say so.
